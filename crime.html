Crime and safety

Reported crime in the United States is near its lowest level since the 1970s, but the national average hides a wide spread: the most dangerous state reports more than seven times the violent crime rate of the safest.

How to read crime statistics. These are offences reported to police, not offences committed. Reporting rates differ by state and by offence — victim surveys consistently find that roughly half of violent crime is never reported — and the FBI's transition to the NIBRS reporting standard left some agencies missing from recent years, so state-to-state comparisons carry real uncertainty. Rates are also population-weighted totals: a state figure blends its cities and its rural counties, which usually differ more from each other than states do. The District of Columbia is a single city and is not comparable to a whole state.
